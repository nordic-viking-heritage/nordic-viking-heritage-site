# SALME — EARLY VIKING AGE RESEARCH DOSSIER

Status: PRIMARY-SOURCE DOSSIER
Project: Nordic Viking Heritage — The People Behind the Viking Age

## Why Salme matters

Margaryan et al. (Nature 2020), *Population genomics of the Viking world*, identifies Salme II in present-day Estonia as an Early Viking Age ship-burial site. The study describes an expedition represented by men who died violently and were buried in boats with high-status weaponry. It notes that the burial predates the documented raid on Lindisfarne in 793 by nearly half a century.

The genomic study reports kinship among the Salme dead: analysis of 34 individuals revealed four brothers buried side by side and a third-degree relative of one of those brothers. The authors use Salme as direct evidence that a Viking expedition could include close family members.

## Evidence boundaries

- Do not claim that VK486 or VK492 was one of the four brothers unless the original kinship table specifically establishes that relationship.
- Do not identify a particular archaeological individual as a commander, king, farmer, warrior rank, or named historical person without primary archaeological evidence.
- Genetic similarity or a commercial historical match does not establish direct descent from an ancient individual.
- The project may describe the Salme group as an Early Viking Age expedition / ship-burial group where supported by the study, while preserving uncertainty over whether its purpose was raiding, diplomacy, or another expeditionary activity.
