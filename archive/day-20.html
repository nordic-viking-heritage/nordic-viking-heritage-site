OUR SAGA — DAY 20

THE SHORE GREW QUIET BEHIND THEM

One by one, the fires of Newfoundland disappeared into the darkness. Red-and-white sails filled above the fleet as 94 Vikings returned to the sea.

Ahead lay Nova Scotia. Hákon stood beneath the sail and watched the horizon. Þóra would remember what came next.

CHAPTER III HAD BEGUN. ONE CREW. ONE SAGA.

HISTORY — DAY 20

THE CLUE THAT POINTED SOUTH

Archaeology at L'Anse aux Meadows preserves evidence that the Norse travelled beyond their Newfoundland base. Butternuts and butternut wood found there point to travel farther south, although they do not identify an exact destination.

THE SAGA MAY BE WILD. THE HISTORY MUST BE TRUE.

MYTH & SAGA — DAY 20

THE WIND DOES NOT BELONG TO THE CAPTAIN

Among the gods was Njǫrðr, associated with the sea, the wind and the fortunes of those who sailed upon them.

Hákon could choose the course. The crew could pull the oars. But the wind belonged to powers older than either.

AND TONIGHT, IT WAS BLOWING SOUTH.

NIGHT WATCH — DAY 20

THE SEA HAD GONE QUIET

Behind the fleet, Newfoundland had vanished into darkness. Most of the crew slept. Only the night watch remained awake.

Far beyond the reach of the lanterns, something was changing. Not a storm. Not yet.
