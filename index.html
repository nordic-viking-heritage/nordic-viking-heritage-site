HISTORY — DAY 27

THE LAND WAS NEVER EMPTY

This is Mi'kma'ki — the traditional and ancestral homeland of the Mi'kmaq. Indigenous peoples had lived in this region for thousands of years before Norse sails crossed the western Atlantic.

Around 1,000 years ago, the Norse also reached North America. L'Anse aux Meadows proves their presence, while the Vinland sagas describe encounters with Indigenous peoples whom the Norse called Skrælingar. Were they Mi'kmaq? We do not know.

HISTORY BEFORE LEGEND. LISTEN BEFORE WE SAIL.

⚡ MYTH & SAGA — DAY 27 ⚡

WHEN TWO WORLDS MET

Now we leave archaeology behind and enter the Vinland sagas. They tell of Thorfinn Karlsefni and his people meeting Indigenous inhabitants whom the Norse called Skrælingar — first through trade, then through fear, misunderstanding and conflict.

How much happened exactly as the sagas describe? We cannot know.

HERE HISTORY FADES — AND THE SAGA BEGINS.

THE NORTH ATLANTIC CROSSING IS COMPLETE

For fifteen days, we watched the horizon.

We left Greenland behind and pushed westward across the cold North Atlantic — one step, one sailor, one day at a time.

Tonight, there is land beneath our feet.
